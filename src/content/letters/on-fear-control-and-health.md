#### 💭 On Fear and Control

There’s a moment we all face—standing at the edge of a decision, gripped by fear, frozen by uncertainty.

And it’s in moments like these that the dialogue between Arjun and Krishna from the _Bhagavad Gita_ still hits hard:

* * *

**अर्जुन:** आपको डर नहीं लग रहा है?

**Arjun:** Aren’t you afraid?

**कृष्ण:** अर्जुन, भविष्य में क्या होने वाला है, उसका किसी को पता नहीं। और जो हुआ ही नहीं, उसकी चिंता किसलिए?

**Krishna:** Arjun, no one knows what the future holds. So why worry about something that hasn’t even happened?

**अर्जुन:** अपने आप पर ऐसा नियंत्रण?

**Arjun:** Such control over yourself?

**कृष्ण:** कर्म करो अर्जुन। जय-पराजय का विचार छोड़कर अपने कर्तव्य पर ध्यान दो। भय अपने आप दूर हो जाएगा।

**Krishna:** Do your duty, Arjun. Let go of thoughts of victory or defeat. Focus only on your action—fear will dissolve on its own.

* * *

Krishna’s answer is not cold detachment—it’s clarity. A sharp reminder that fear often comes from trying to control what hasn’t even happened yet.

We obsess over outcomes: What if I fail? What if things don’t go the way I planned? But Krishna flips the focus. He says: **Do your work. Let go of the result.** That doesn’t mean we stop caring. It means we stop trying to predict the future before we’ve taken the first step.

And maybe that’s the real superpower—not fearlessness, but presence. Doing what’s in front of you with your whole self. No guarantees, no perfect outcomes. Just the courage to act anyway.

What would happen if you stopped worrying about what might go wrong and just focused on showing up, fully, for what’s yours to do?

#### 💭 On the Four Horsemen of Death

In the book _Outlive_, Dr. Peter Attia talks about something he calls “The Four Horsemen of Death.” Sounds dramatic, but it’s uncomfortably real.

These four major killers are the slow, silent forces behind most early deaths today:

*   **Cardiovascular Disease:** Heart attacks, strokes, and blood vessel issues. Often invisible until it’s too late.
    
*   **Cancer:** Unchecked cell growth. Sometimes fast, sometimes creeping in slowly over years.
    
*   **Neurodegenerative Diseases:** Like Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s—these don’t just affect memory, but identity itself.
    
*   **Metabolic Dysfunction:** Think type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation—problems tied to how we eat, sleep, and move (or don’t).
    

What’s striking is that **most of these aren't sudden.** They build over time—quietly, patiently. Through daily habits, routines, or neglect. That’s what makes them dangerous. You can’t fight what you’re not even aware of.

Attia’s message isn’t fear-based. It’s empowering. These are not just fates to accept—they're challenges we can anticipate and prepare for. Prevention isn’t glamorous. It’s walking more. Sleeping better. Managing stress. Choosing foods that fuel instead of fog.

In a world obsessed with short-term results, this book is a reminder: **play the long game.** The real win isn’t just to live longer—it’s to live better, with strength, clarity, and vitality.
